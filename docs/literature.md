# Verified literature and its role in the draft

Bibliographic metadata were checked against publisher or author/institutional
records on October 10, 2026. The bibliography uses issue years where available,
which sometimes differ from online-first dates. The entries are in
[`ms/references.bib`](../ms/references.bib).

## How large are scandal penalties?

- [Basinger (2013)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1065912912451144),
  *Scandals and Congressional Elections in the Post-Watergate Era*, documents
  electoral losses and departures. Its outcomes show why surviving officials'
  approval alone is an incomplete measure of accountability.
- [Praino and Stockemer (2022)](https://academic.oup.com/pa/article-abstract/75/3/469/6299982),
  *The Electoral Consequences of Scandals: A Meta-Analysis*, finds generally
  adverse electoral consequences; it does not license a single approval-point
  benchmark across studies.
- [von Sikorski (2018)](https://ijoc.org/index.php/ijoc/article/view/7100),
  *The Aftermath of Political Scandals: A Meta-Analysis*, emphasizes differences
  across scandals, prior attitudes and contexts, and weak measurement of news
  intensity. This is directly relevant to the mixed categories in our inventory.

## Partisan protection and its limits

- [Anduiza, Gallego, and Muñoz (2013)](https://portalrecerca.uab.cat/ca/publications/turning-a-blind-eye-experimental-evidence-of-partisan-bias-in-att/)
  experimentally examines partisan bias in judgments of corruption in Spain.
- [Funck and McCabe (2022)](https://www.researchwithrutgers.org/en/publications/partisanship-information-and-the-conditional-effects-of-scandal-o/)
  uses candidate conjoint experiments. Voters can judge misconduct negatively
  while continuing to prefer a candidate's party or policies. Continued support
  is not synonymous with a favorable moral judgment.
- [Lee et al. (2023)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/pops.12882)
  studies partisan forgiveness in the Trudeau blackface controversy; the setting
  supports examining partisan strength and electoral stakes.
- [Onishi (2026)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/20531680261428570)
  studies Britain's Partygate using an unexpected event during survey fieldwork.
  Governing-party supporters also reduce support; opposition evaluations start
  low. This is a relevant counterexample to universal co-partisan immunity.
- [Wolsky (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1017/XPS.2020.36)
  experimentally studies hypocrisy, resignation, and party evaluations. Its
  individual-scandal-to-party-evaluation link complements our separate experiment
  about how ordinary partisans respond to their leaders' controversies.

The existing Stoker (1993), Puglisi and Snyder (2011), and Eggers (2014) entries
remain relevant for prior attitudes, selective coverage, and partisan electoral
tradeoffs respectively. The local Ahler, Roush, and Sood research note is cited
as unpublished companion work, not as an independently published replication.

## Publicity and competing news

- [Eisensee and Strömberg (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1162/qjec.122.2.693)
  uses competing news, including the Olympics, to study disaster coverage and
  US relief decisions. This is the direct Olympics crowdout precedent; it is
  not a study of scandal approval penalties.
- [Nyhan (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007123413000458)
  connects news congestion with presidential media scandal. It studies scandal
  emergence/coverage, not the causal approval response conditional on a scandal.
- [Nyhan (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1177/1065912916684034)
  extends the question to governors, using neighboring-state severe weather and
  overlapping media markets. It is especially useful for our state-level setting.
- [Durante and Zhuravskaya (2018)](https://research.lancaster-university.uk/en/publications/attack-when-the-world-is-not-watching-us-media-and-the-israeli-pa/)
  finds strategic timing of attacks around competing US news. Its relevance is
  to the assumption that revelation timing cannot be chosen strategically.
- [Hayes (2025)](https://www.dannyhayes.org/uploads/6/9/8/5/69858539/the_local_news_crisis_and_political_scandal.pdf)
  studies declining local scandal reporting and its association with political
  sanctions. Its stricter misconduct inventory offers a useful external
  comparison for our broad catalog. This is not an Olympics IV design.

The [publicity design note](publicity-design.md) translates these studies into a
implemented broadcast/NAES analysis and preserves the earlier approval-cohort
support check.

## Timing and persistence

Three additional publisher-verified entries are Mitchell (2014), on the timing,
repetition and decay of scandal information in a panel experiment; Warner (2023),
on daily attention and impulse responses across Twitter groups; and Rottinghaus
(2014), on executive-scandal duration. The [dynamics note](scandal-dynamics.md)
links these studies, separates their estimands, and develops a recommendation
matched to the resolution of our approval data. Onishi's seven-day window around
the Partygate video is also useful: the video is a later information shock within
an existing episode, not its first public report.
