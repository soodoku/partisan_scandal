# The archived experiment and `partisan_principled`

The experimental respondent data are available. The original experimental manuscript describes the November 2013 MTurk study in the companion `partisan_principled` repository: 930 respondents, Obama and the health exchange rollout, Cruz and the shutdown, and a Rob Ford placebo. The five assignment fields, item battery, party-strength branches, and sample counts match. More decisively, the public data reproduce every coefficient of the archived interaction table to its displayed precision.

## Frozen inputs and reproduction

`data/external/principled/mturk.csv` is the companion's public deidentified export, containing 930 rows and 67 retained columns. Its original source is `mturk_as/partisanresponse.csv`, whose recorded source SHA256 is `7f3114951435495220bdd7b9b7c84fc1c588bfef8ab23baf8d14c1664ab20089`. This is the original-file hash, not the public-export hash. The frozen public-export hash and the hash of its copied recoding helper are recorded separately in `data/external/principled/manifest.csv`. No private input or runtime sibling dependency is required. Run `Rscript scripts/08_experiment.R` from this repository.

The archived paper averages seven favorable trait responses, excluding patriotism: ignorant, sincere, open to reason, smug, selfish, compassionate, and hypocritical. Negative traits are reversed. Each party index is placed on 0–100; the raw own-minus-opposition gap ranges from −100 to 100. The archived regression scale is `(raw_gap + 100) / 200`. This bridge reports that archived measure multiplied by 100, `50 + raw_gap / 2`, so its polarization scale is 0–100. A five-point change in this rescaled polarization measure is a ten-point change in the raw trait gap. It is not a five-point change in either party's thermometer or a politician's approval rating.

Available-item means reproduce the archived regression. Both parties have all seven items for 745 of 746 analyzed respondents. The remaining record has an observed available-item index; it is not filled with zero. The all-assigned sample contains 746 partisans, including leaners, of whom 226 are strong and 520 weak/leaning. These counts reproduce the archived table; the manuscript's prose count of 726 is inconsistent with that table. The strict pretreatment attention screen used in the companion leaves 724 (223 strong, 501 weak).

## What reproduces

`tabs/experiment_archived_model.csv` reports the saturated arm-by-strength regression, with both the original OLS covariance and HC3. All-assigned coefficients divided by 100 reproduce archived Table 1 column 4. In particular, the strong-partisan interaction for out-party nonresponse relative to the Ford placebo is 11.203 rescaled polarization points (original SE 3.382, p=.00097; HC3 SE 3.600, 95% CI [4.135, 18.271], p=.00193). With the strict attention screen, this interaction remains 10.994 points (HC3 CI [3.836, 18.153], p=.00266).

This is a difference between strong and weak respondents in the nonresponse-versus-placebo effect. The placebo changes the leader, scandal, and country as well as the presence of American party cues. It is not the direct comparison of the same Obama/Cruz story with versus without a partisan approval decline.

`tabs/experiment_bridge.csv` reports both direct comparisons for every strength group, both attention samples, pooled and party-standardized cell estimates, the rescaled and raw gaps, and the separate own-party, opposing-party, and warmth outcomes. All displayed p-values are two-sided and unadjusted. These recovered subgroup analyses are exploratory; no preregistration is available. Fixed party shares come from each eligible subgroup for subgroup effects; moderation contrasts use one common eligible-sample party composition for strong and weak respondents. Welch–Satterthwaite intervals allow unequal cell variances. The CSV also provides HC3 saturated-cell contrasts with residual degrees of freedom equal to the number of compared observations minus cells; the manuscript table and macros use Welch inference.

For strict-attention strong partisans, party-standardized out-party nonresponse increases rescaled polarization by 10.19 points versus the placebo (95% CI [4.51, 15.86], p=.00062) and by 5.89 versus out-party response ([0.55, 11.23], p=.0312). The direct nonresponse-versus-response effect differs between strong and weak partisans by 5.95 points ([-0.64, 12.55], p=.0764); the nonresponse-versus-placebo effect differs by 12.2 points ([5.3, 19.1], p<.001). Thus the archived moderation is much clearer for the placebo contrast than for the direct response manipulation. The unstandardized direct strong-partisan effect is 5.48 points ([-0.28, 11.23], p=.0618), and is identical across attention samples because that screen drops no strong respondents in these two arms.

The direct strong-partisan polarization effect partly reflects improved views of the respondent's own side. Under strict attention and party standardization, nonresponse versus response increases own-party favorable traits by 8.94 points ([2.30, 15.59]) and decreases opposition favorable traits by 2.83 points ([-8.98, 3.32]). Opposition warmth changes by −5.48 points ([-14.92, 3.95]). It would overstate the direct evidence to describe the polarization result solely as a precisely estimated deterioration in views of opponents.

## Why the companion's headline differs

The companion's primary MTurk estimand is the all-partisan, party-standardized warmth response to declining rather than sustained approval, with strict pretreatment attention screening. Its own-party estimate is −4.33 points (95% CI [−8.89, 0.22]); its opposition estimate is +1.20 ([−4.10, 6.51]); the difference is +5.54 ([−1.44, 12.52]). These are neither the archived seven-trait polarization outcome nor its strong-partisan subgroup. The companion's auxiliary MTurk trait index also includes all eight items, whereas the archive excludes patriotism.

The 2018 YouGov study is separate evidence about portraits of fair-minded opponents and biased allies. Its main fair-minded-opposition trait effect is +2.53 points ([−0.31, 5.37]) and warmth effect +0.51 ([−2.84, 3.86]). Missing factorial cells prevent a same-behavior, ally-versus-opponent interaction. Neither experiment estimates the effect of real scandal exposure on elected officials' approval; they examine downstream evaluations of partisan citizens or parties.

## Validation

The companion's existing tests passed locally (57 assertions, no failures, warnings, or skips), and its R lint returned no issues. No companion file was edited. The bridge's local tests verify archived coefficients and standard errors, sample counts, outcome scaling, direct Welch estimates against `t.test`, the HC3 baseline variance against its closed-form saturated-cell expression, and the strong-minus-weak contrast and variance against independent subgroup estimates. Source fingerprints are recorded in the frozen-input manifest. No results were chosen by searching over outcomes or screens: the seven-item measure and strong-PID contrast come directly from the archived paper, and both the archived and existing companion attention rules are retained.
